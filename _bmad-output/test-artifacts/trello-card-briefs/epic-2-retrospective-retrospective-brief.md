# Retrospective Brief: Epic 2 Retrospective

## What This Retrospective Is
Capture what worked, what changed, and what to improve after Epic 2 is complete.

## Current Delivery State
- Retrospective state: Done
- Overall project progress: 34/34 stories done

## Explicit Project Detail
- Card intent: Capture what the epic taught us and what must carry forward into the next one.
- Intended outcome: Epic 2 was meant to turn the governed publication spine from Epic 1 into a small, trustworthy public website with clear route ownership, readable navigation, and enough launch-ready support structure to carry the case study.
- Completed in the epic: Story 2.1 established the shared Astro shell, navigation, and four-route public spine.
- Completed in the epic: Story 2.2 gave the landing page a real orientation role instead of a starter-template placeholder.
- Completed in the epic: Story 2.3 turned the case-study route into the primary proof entry and introduced the portrait-based author cue.
- Completed in the epic: Story 2.4 established the technical-method route as the bounded engineering-proof layer.
- What went well: Route responsibility stayed clear. Landing, case study, technical method, and contact each ended up with one primary job instead of drifting into a generic portfolio catalog.
- What went well: The public site remained static-first and lightweight. The team resisted adding unnecessary runtime behavior before the analytical story was ready.
- What went well: Shared layout discipline from Story 2.1 made later route work faster. Once the shell and route spine were stable, content work moved with much less friction.
- Harder than expected: Copy and tone were harder than shell implementation. It was easy to produce pages that were structurally correct but still sounded too abstract, too tool-centered, or too AI-generated.
- Harder than expected: The technical-method route required careful restraint. It needed to feel credible to technical reviewers without turning into a documentation portal.
- Harder than expected: Minimal legal/contact readiness was conceptually simple but still needed explicit bounded choices so the site did not accidentally imply a lead-capture workflow or a broader operational surface.
- Action item: Product direction: Preserve explicit route ownership so later analytical and technical additions do not blur the responsibilities of landing, case study, and technical method.
- Action item: Content and UX: Continue refining tone toward senior-analyst credibility rather than Fabric-first or manifesto-style language.
- Action item: Engineering: Keep the site static-first and lightweight unless a later story has a strong reason to add more interactivity.
- Action item: Delivery discipline: Treat copy and visual refinement as intentional follow-up work rather than trying to polish every route prematurely.
- Closing takeaway: Epic 2 successfully created a credible public shell for the project. Its most important result was not just that the routes exist, but that the site now has a disciplined reading sequence and a believable public posture. That gave Epic 3 a stable place to turn the case into an actual analytical product.
