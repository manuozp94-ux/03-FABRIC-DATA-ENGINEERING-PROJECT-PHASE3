# Retrospective Brief: Epic 4 Retrospective

## What This Retrospective Is
Capture what worked, what changed, and what to improve after Epic 4 is complete.

## Current Delivery State
- Retrospective state: Done
- Overall project progress: 34/34 stories done

## Explicit Project Detail
- Card intent: Capture what the epic taught us and what must carry forward into the next one.
- Intended outcome: Epic 4 was meant to prove that the project had real technical discipline behind the web experience: a bounded source slice, explicit masking and evidence boundaries, model and grain explanation, and one Fabric or Power BI proof surface that stayed subordinate to the site.
- Completed in the epic: Story 4.1 explained the approved source slice and the three-zone boundary across private preparation, governed analytics, and public delivery.
- Completed in the epic: Story 4.2 documented masking, abstraction, simplifications, and public-safe treatment categories.
- Completed in the epic: Story 4.3 tied modeled entities and grain back to business questions instead of leaving the model as generic schema talk.
- Completed in the epic: Story 4.4 assembled the bounded technical evidence bundle and labeled direct, indirect, and deferred proof coverage.
- What went well: The technical route stopped being abstract. It became a real explanation layer with source scope, model logic, validation posture, and bounded platform evidence.
- What went well: Boundary discipline held up under pressure. The project proved that it could show real technical rigor without collapsing into a raw repo tour or exposing private-prep leverage.
- What went well: The Fabric proof path became concrete. The Lakehouse, semantic model, report shell, and embed path all moved from concept to visible bounded proof.
- Harder than expected: It was harder than expected to explain technical rigor without sounding either evasive or overly tool-centric.
- Harder than expected: The term "Bronze" created confusion because the project had two valid meanings in play: messy upstream realism and the proof-side landed copy of governed data.
- Harder than expected: The first Fabric proof surfaced a useful tension: the platform path was real, but the business logic underneath it was still too provisional to justify heavy UI iteration.
- Action item: Product direction: Keep the logic-first principle explicit so report composition never outruns data credibility.
- Action item: Data generation design: Turn controlled realism, business rules, and inventory-loop behavior into executable controls rather than leaving them as narrative intent.
- Action item: Technical method: Continue tightening language when terms like Bronze, proof, current, candidate, and approved package carry different meanings in different layers.
- Action item: Delivery discipline: Keep review focused on overclaim risk, boundary drift, and whether the technical route stays aligned with the actual live proof state.
- Closing takeaway: Epic 4 succeeded because it turned technical credibility into something inspectable without breaking the public-safe boundary. The biggest win was not only the live Fabric proof shell, but the fact that the project learned how to explain architecture, evidence, and limits honestly. That set up Epic 5 to focus on logic quality instead of platform theater.
