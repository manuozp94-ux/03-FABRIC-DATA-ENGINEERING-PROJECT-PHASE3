# Retrospective Brief: Epic 3 Retrospective

## What This Retrospective Is
Capture what worked, what changed, and what to improve after Epic 3 is complete.

## Current Delivery State
- Retrospective state: Done
- Overall project progress: 34/34 stories done

## Explicit Project Detail
- Card intent: Capture what the epic taught us and what must carry forward into the next one.
- Intended outcome: Epic 3 was meant to turn the case-study route from a proof entry into a real analytical product: one bounded supply-chain decision view, one supporting cross-functional read, and visible freshness context that made the public slice trustworthy.
- Completed in the epic: Story 3.1 framed the business problem, team tensions, and intended reading sequence in business language.
- Completed in the epic: Story 3.2 established the primary supply-chain decision view with bounded KPIs, policy signals, and a short action queue.
- Completed in the epic: Story 3.3 added a supporting demand-pressure view showing concentration by account pattern, product family, and current-month weight.
- Completed in the epic: Story 3.4 added freshness and approved-package context so the case reads as governed release output rather than live operations.
- What went well: The project found a clearer thesis mid-epic and actually used it. Reframing the product as supply-chain decision support immediately made the case easier to reason about.
- What went well: The main analytical hierarchy became much stronger: one primary view, one supporting demand view, and freshness as trust context instead of another dashboard.
- What went well: Review remained useful. The strongest corrections improved semantics and analytical defensibility rather than only surface presentation.
- Harder than expected: Scope pressure was real. The project kept trying to turn into sales analytics, logistics decisioning, and planning logic all at once.
- Harder than expected: KPI naming and explanation needed more care than expected. A metric can compile and still be misleading if it overclaims what the data actually proves.
- Harder than expected: The public policy layer required honesty. Some useful reading rules were derived in the route itself, but that had to be labeled clearly so the page did not pretend those were governed contract fields.
- Action item: Product direction: Keep the supply-chain decision-support thesis explicit across Epic 4 so technical proof reinforces the product instead of redefining it.
- Action item: Technical method: Explain source domains, masking, grain, and evidence through bounded public artifacts rather than through generic tool talk.
- Action item: Content and UX: Continue reducing any copy that sounds like platform evangelism or portfolio philosophy instead of real analytical explanation.
- Action item: Delivery discipline: Keep review focused on analytical truthfulness, boundary honesty, and what the public slice can actually support.
- Closing takeaway: Epic 3 was the turning point of the project. It resolved the biggest ambiguity in the product by deciding what the tool is actually for and what it is not. The most valuable outcome was not just the new route sections, but the stronger analytical thesis behind them. That gives Epic 4 a much better foundation for technical proof.
