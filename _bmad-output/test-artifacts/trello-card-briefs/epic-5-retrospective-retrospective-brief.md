# Retrospective Brief: Epic 5 Retrospective

## What This Retrospective Is
Capture what worked, what changed, and what to improve after Epic 5 is complete.

## Current Delivery State
- Retrospective state: Done
- Overall project progress: 34/34 stories done

## Explicit Project Detail
- Card intent: Capture what the epic taught us and what must carry forward into the next one.
- Intended outcome: Epic 5 was meant to shift the project from a proof shell into a logic-first operating path: deterministic business rules, coherent incremental growth, representative masked entities, coherence gates, and a Fabric proof stack refreshed from stabilized serving logic instead of from manual report iteration.
- Completed in the epic: Story 5.1 defined the masked industry frame, business-rule parameters, generation invariants, and the internal control tables needed for logic-driven growth.
- Completed in the epic: Story 5.2 materialized internal generation controls, pending receipt and shock state, and the first controlled candidate refresh path.
- Completed in the epic: Story 5.3 expanded the tiny seed slice into a representative bounded market with `96` accounts, `140` products, `16` locations, and governed eligibility and stocking coverage.
- Completed in the epic: Story 5.4 added the representative coherence gate so realism, invariants, and traceability are checked before promotion.
- What went well: The project made a real priority correction instead of pushing ahead with premature report work. That was one of the healthiest decisions in the epic.
- What went well: The logic-first workflow became executable, not just conceptual. Internal controls, representative population, coherence checks, and proof refresh all now exist as governed steps.
- What went well: The project got more honest about its own gaps. Discovering that the seed market was too small led to a redesign of the data path rather than a cosmetic workaround.
- Harder than expected: The original seed slice was far too small for believable analysis, and that was not obvious early enough.
- Harder than expected: Several concepts that sounded simple in conversation needed sharper operational distinction in practice: masking versus population expansion, Bronze versus upstream realism, and proof shell versus logic stability.
- Harder than expected: Story sequencing had to be corrected mid-epic. Representative entity expansion had to happen before deeper realism review, not after it.
- Action item: Release operations: Add explicit release identifiers, last-known-good references, and recovery-ready records around the representative and Fabric proof paths.
- Action item: Change communication: Publish short, governed change summaries whenever proof scope, evidence coverage, or live artifacts materially change.
- Action item: Documentation discipline: Keep runbooks, public-route language, and manifests synchronized whenever the live Fabric path changes.
- Action item: Product direction: Continue resisting UI-first pressure until release operations are strong enough that proof changes remain understandable and recoverable.
- Closing takeaway: Epic 5 was the epic where the project grew up operationally. It corrected a misleadingly small seed universe, turned business logic into governed generation controls, and proved that the Fabric surface can now follow stabilized serving logic instead of guessing ahead of it. The next challenge is no longer whether the logic-first path exists, but whether releases around that path can become equally disciplined and recoverable.
