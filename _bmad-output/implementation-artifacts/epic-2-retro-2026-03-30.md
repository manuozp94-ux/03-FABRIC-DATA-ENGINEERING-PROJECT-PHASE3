# Epic 2 Retrospective: Build the Public Site Shell and Route Spine

Date: 2026-03-30
Epic Status: done
Retrospective Status: done

## Epic Review

### Intended Outcome

Epic 2 was meant to turn the governed publication spine from Epic 1 into a small, trustworthy public website with clear route ownership, readable navigation, and enough launch-ready support structure to carry the case study.

### What We Completed

- Story 2.1 established the shared Astro shell, navigation, and four-route public spine.
- Story 2.2 gave the landing page a real orientation role instead of a starter-template placeholder.
- Story 2.3 turned the case-study route into the primary proof entry and introduced the portrait-based author cue.
- Story 2.4 established the technical-method route as the bounded engineering-proof layer.
- Story 2.5 completed contact, privacy, and terms routes with an intentionally minimal public-support posture.

### What Went Well

- Route responsibility stayed clear. Landing, case study, technical method, and contact each ended up with one primary job instead of drifting into a generic portfolio catalog.
- The public site remained static-first and lightweight. The team resisted adding unnecessary runtime behavior before the analytical story was ready.
- Shared layout discipline from Story 2.1 made later route work faster. Once the shell and route spine were stable, content work moved with much less friction.
- The project started to feel human. The portrait, business-first framing, and contact route made the site read more like a professional analytical product and less like a generic template.

### What Was Harder Than Expected

- Copy and tone were harder than shell implementation. It was easy to produce pages that were structurally correct but still sounded too abstract, too tool-centered, or too AI-generated.
- The technical-method route required careful restraint. It needed to feel credible to technical reviewers without turning into a documentation portal.
- Minimal legal/contact readiness was conceptually simple but still needed explicit bounded choices so the site did not accidentally imply a lead-capture workflow or a broader operational surface.

### Key Discoveries

- A small route spine is a strength, not a limitation. It made the project easier to understand and easier to evolve.
- Tone and reading sequence matter as much as layout. The strongest improvements came from deciding what each route should help the reader do, not from adding more sections.
- The public surface should stay web-first. The website worked best when it framed the analytical case instead of trying to impersonate a BI tool too early.
- Copy refinement deserves its own backlog. Trying to perfect editorial tone before the case and visuals existed would have created unnecessary rework.

### Review Patterns

- The only meaningful review finding in the epic was navigational rather than visual: active-route matching in the shared navigation needed a trailing-slash-safe fix.
- Most risk in Epic 2 came from language drift and route sprawl, not from framework complexity.
- The best corrections happened when copy was rewritten to reduce manifesto language and tool-first framing.

### Technical Debt and Open Risks

- The public copy is still functional rather than fully refined. That is now tracked separately in the copy and visual refinement backlog.
- The routes are structurally sound, but later visual refinement will still be needed once the final analytical objects settle.
- Trello synchronization is useful for visibility, but it should not be mistaken for the final editorial surface for route content.

## Next Epic Preparation

### Epic 3 Readiness

Epic 3 was ready because Epic 2 established the route spine clearly enough that the case-study page could stop acting like a landing page and start acting like an analytical product.

### What Epic 3 Needed to Preserve

- Keep the case-study route as the primary business-proof surface.
- Treat analytics as the center of the experience, not decoration on top of a website shell.
- Avoid letting the public site drift into either a generic marketing site or a documentation dump.
- Keep copy sober and business-first as the analytical views come online.

### Action Items

- Owner: Product direction
  Preserve explicit route ownership so later analytical and technical additions do not blur the responsibilities of landing, case study, and technical method.

- Owner: Content and UX
  Continue refining tone toward senior-analyst credibility rather than Fabric-first or manifesto-style language.

- Owner: Engineering
  Keep the site static-first and lightweight unless a later story has a strong reason to add more interactivity.

- Owner: Delivery discipline
  Treat copy and visual refinement as intentional follow-up work rather than trying to polish every route prematurely.

### Carry-Forward Agreements

- The public web layer remains the primary storytelling surface.
- Power BI or Fabric proof, if exposed later, should stay subordinate to the curated website.
- Each route should preserve one primary responsibility in the reading sequence.
- Public professionalism matters, but business clarity matters more than ornamental polish.

## Closing Summary

Epic 2 successfully created a credible public shell for the project. Its most important result was not just that the routes exist, but that the site now has a disciplined reading sequence and a believable public posture. That gave Epic 3 a stable place to turn the case into an actual analytical product.
