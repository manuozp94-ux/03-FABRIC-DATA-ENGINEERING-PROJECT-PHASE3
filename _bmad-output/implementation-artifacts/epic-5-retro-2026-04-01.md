# Epic 5 Retrospective: Operationalize Logic-First Fabric Serving

Date: 2026-04-01
Epic Status: done
Retrospective Status: done

## Epic Review

### Intended Outcome

Epic 5 was meant to shift the project from a proof shell into a logic-first operating path: deterministic business rules, coherent incremental growth, representative masked entities, coherence gates, and a Fabric proof stack refreshed from stabilized serving logic instead of from manual report iteration.

### What We Completed

- Story 5.1 defined the masked industry frame, business-rule parameters, generation invariants, and the internal control tables needed for logic-driven growth.
- Story 5.2 materialized internal generation controls, pending receipt and shock state, and the first controlled candidate refresh path.
- Story 5.3 expanded the tiny seed slice into a representative bounded market with `96` accounts, `140` products, `16` locations, and governed eligibility and stocking coverage.
- Story 5.4 added the representative coherence gate so realism, invariants, and traceability are checked before promotion.
- Story 5.5 refreshed the live Fabric proof stack from the coherence-approved representative package and confirmed all `16` bounded proof tables, the semantic model, and the report shell.

### What Went Well

- The project made a real priority correction instead of pushing ahead with premature report work. That was one of the healthiest decisions in the epic.
- The logic-first workflow became executable, not just conceptual. Internal controls, representative population, coherence checks, and proof refresh all now exist as governed steps.
- The project got more honest about its own gaps. Discovering that the seed market was too small led to a redesign of the data path rather than a cosmetic workaround.
- Fabric credibility improved because it now reflects stabilized serving logic and a representative market rather than a smoke-scale seed slice.

### What Was Harder Than Expected

- The original seed slice was far too small for believable analysis, and that was not obvious early enough.
- Several concepts that sounded simple in conversation needed sharper operational distinction in practice: masking versus population expansion, Bronze versus upstream realism, and proof shell versus logic stability.
- Story sequencing had to be corrected mid-epic. Representative entity expansion had to happen before deeper realism review, not after it.
- The live Fabric refresh path required patience and careful verification because the notebook-run and patching steps are slower and easier to over-assume than local generation.

### Key Discoveries

- Masking preserves identity safety; it does not create representative entity diversity. Those are different jobs and need different layers.
- The right unit of progress was not "more rows" but "more believable operating behavior."
- A coherence gate matters because structural validity alone is too weak once the project starts generating behavior over time.
- `fact_inventory_inbound` was the right addition for the MVP. It closed the inventory loop enough to make snapshots feel calculated instead of arbitrary.
- The project now has a better understanding of when Fabric proof work is worth doing: after logic stabilizes, not before.

### Review Patterns

- The strongest review moments happened when we questioned realism and traceability rather than code syntax.
- Good corrections often came from reframing the problem. The biggest example was realizing the issue was not broken masking, but an undersized entity population.
- The most valuable review habit was checking whether the repo language still matched what the system was actually doing after scope corrections and live refreshes.

### Technical Debt and Open Risks

- Release operations are now the obvious next gap. The project can generate, validate, and refresh, but it still needs stronger release identity, change-summary, and recovery discipline in Epic 6.
- The representative market is much better than the seed slice, but it is still a bounded model and will need ongoing care so rule drift does not erode credibility.
- Some wording in docs and manifests had to be corrected after the live refresh path evolved. That is a reminder that repo narratives can go stale even when the system works.
- Desktop-authored report polish is still intentionally deferred, so the current report remains technically credible before it becomes visually final.

## Next Epic Preparation

### Epic 6 Readiness

Epic 6 is ready because the project now has something meaningful to operate: a coherence-approved representative package, a live Fabric proof stack, and a logic-first refresh path that deserves proper release discipline.

### What Epic 6 Needed to Preserve

- Keep promotion fail-closed and traceable.
- Preserve the distinction between candidate, representative, approved, and live proof artifacts.
- Keep release records tied to actual validation and refresh outputs instead of relying on memory.
- Maintain the current rule that richer Desktop work remains downstream of stable data and stable release state.

### Action Items

- Owner: Release operations
  Add explicit release identifiers, last-known-good references, and recovery-ready records around the representative and Fabric proof paths.

- Owner: Change communication
  Publish short, governed change summaries whenever proof scope, evidence coverage, or live artifacts materially change.

- Owner: Documentation discipline
  Keep runbooks, public-route language, and manifests synchronized whenever the live Fabric path changes.

- Owner: Product direction
  Continue resisting UI-first pressure until release operations are strong enough that proof changes remain understandable and recoverable.

### Carry-Forward Agreements

- Representative coherence approval stays mandatory before any live Fabric refresh.
- The live Fabric proof remains bounded, public-safe, and subordinate to the curated web experience.
- Synthetic growth and representative expansion must stay deterministic and reviewable.
- Release truthfulness matters as much as analytical plausibility.

## Closing Summary

Epic 5 was the epic where the project grew up operationally. It corrected a misleadingly small seed universe, turned business logic into governed generation controls, and proved that the Fabric surface can now follow stabilized serving logic instead of guessing ahead of it. The next challenge is no longer whether the logic-first path exists, but whether releases around that path can become equally disciplined and recoverable.
